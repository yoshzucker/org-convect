#+title: org-convect
#+options: toc:2

The Horizons of Focus above the project, in Org.

* Why

Org holds projects and next actions well.  Everything above them — the areas
being maintained, the goals they serve, the vision behind those, the purpose
behind that — has nowhere to live, and so tends not to exist.

Convection stops when there is no difference.  What this package works on is
the difference between what was declared to matter and where the hours
actually went, and it reads in both directions:

- *upward* — the clock says which areas were paid for, and which of them
  nobody ever claimed
- *downward* — the ladder says which goals nothing serves, and which rungs are
  past the review GTD asks for

Heat rises, and what rises then governs what is below.  Those are GTD's two
directions, and they are not the same direction.

What matters most is settled from the top: purpose shapes the vision, the
vision shapes the goals, the goals frame the areas.  And yet the ladder gets
written from the bottom, because it is very hard to reflect on purpose while
drowning in the day.  Both are true.  The file is ordered for the first; the
package asks for the second.

* The model

Two different things are being modelled here, and most confusion about this
package comes from reading them as one.

- the *ladder* holds what you have taken on — commitments, at four altitudes
- *ACT* records how you actually behaved — conduct, which is not a commitment
  and is not in the ladder at all

** The four rungs

Every rung is a heading.  Its body answers one question, and which question
depends on the rung.

| Rung | What it is | Its body answers | Finishes? | Dated? |
|---+---+---+---+---|
| Purpose and Principles | why any of it matters, and the conduct you hold to | what it rules out | never | no |
| Vision | what it is like once it has worked | the picture itself | approached, not reached | no |
| Goals and Objectives | what must become true in a year or two | how you would know | *yes* | ~CONVECT_BY~ |
| Areas of Focus and Accountability | what you are answerable for | the standard | never | no |

Below them are projects and next actions, which belong to
[[https://github.com/yoshzucker/org-foresight][org-foresight]].

** Two directions, and they are not the same one

Derivation runs *down*: purpose drives the vision, the vision creates goals,
goals frame areas.  That is GTD's own account of what the levels mean.

Writing runs *up*: you start from the areas you already carry, because nobody
buried in the day can answer honestly about purpose.

The file is ordered for the first.  ~org-convect-next-rung~ asks for the
second.  Collapsing them into one order loses half the model.

** Every link points up

| | Lives on | Value |
|---+---+---|
| ~CONVECT_SERVES~ | a rung, naming what it is in service of | names of higher rungs, several allowed, always optional |
| ~CONVECT_AREA~ | a task, naming its area | one area's name, inherited by everything beneath |

*Nothing points down.*  A task carries an area's name and asks nothing else of
you, which is why opening a five-minute job costs no thought about purpose.
The ladder is climbed during a review and at no other time.

=CONVECT_AREA= is the one binding that has to be *made*.  Until an entry
carries it, its clocked hours are unattributed — which is the point, and what
=org-convect-unclaimed-time= reports.

Two commands make it:

- =M-x org-convect-set-area= puts a declared area on the entry at point, in an
  Org buffer or from an agenda line, which is where most of it gets decided
- =%(org-convect-read-area)= inside a capture template asks at capture time

*** Why not CATEGORY

It is the obvious slot and this package used it at first, which was a mistake.

CATEGORY is Org's own.  Every entry has one whether you set it or not, users
already put their own meanings in it, and other packages read it — org-calsync
writes what a thing *is* there, org-foresight asks which of *your* categories
mean private.  Note the difference: those two *ask*.  Declaring that CATEGORY
must name an area would have been this package *telling* you what your own
categories are for, in a slot that was never its to take.

There is a second reason, and it is the one that settles it.  Which area a
task serves and what kind of thing it is are independent: a meeting can belong
to engineering or to running the team, and neither answer can be derived from
the other.  A calendar import knows the kind and cannot know the area.  Two
independent questions need two slots, and no amount of splitting the areas
produces the kinds — "meeting" is not something anybody is accountable for.

Inheritance, the reason CATEGORY looked attractive, costs nothing to keep:
=org-entry-get= takes an argument for it, so =CONVECT_AREA= is read the same
way down a subtree without changing =org-use-property-inheritance= or anything
else global.

Only the areas that exist are offered, so work cannot be filed against a
responsibility nobody has claimed.  Where to ask is a judgement: a capture
that starts a clock knows the answer already, and an inbox capture must *not*
ask — catching and deciding are different acts, and an inbox that costs a
decision stops being used.

** Where the ladder stops

#+begin_example
  purpose ──derives──> vision ──> goal ──> area ──> task
     │
     └──governs──> conduct, in the moment
                        ▲
                        └── ACT is here, outside the ladder
#+end_example

The ladder holds commitments.  What a principle produces is not a commitment
lower down — it is behaviour, right now — and behaviour is not in the ladder.

So "nothing in the ladder was derived from this principle" is *not* evidence
that the principle is unused.  The evidence for that lives somewhere the
ladder cannot see.

** What ACT adds

Three things, and all of them come out together with the feature.

1. *Choice points.*  One moment, recorded: what pulled, what you did instead,
   and whether it moved you =towards=, =partly= or =away=.  Written as a child
   of the rung it is about, so no property has to name the value — the parent
   says it.  Usually a principle; any rung is allowed, because the pull is
   sometimes away from a responsibility rather than from a value.

2. *Domains.*  A checklist of life spheres, deliberately independent of the
   areas, asking whether every part of a life has something maintaining it.
   Independence is the whole mechanism: derive the domains from the areas and
   the question becomes true by construction and finds nothing, ever.

3. *Two pieces of evidence the ladder cannot produce.*

   | | What it says | Why the ladder cannot say it |
   |---+---+---|
   | ~org-convect-act-drift~ | a run of =away= against a principle means alignment has gone | GTD puts no calendar on the top two rungs; it visits them "when needed", which is a condition nobody notices from inside |
   | ~org-convect-act-in-use-p~ | a rung with choice points under it is in use | a principle's output is conduct, and conduct is not a rung |

** What each check can and cannot see

The last column is the honest one.

| Check | Sees | *Cannot see* |
|---+---+---|
| ~bare-rung~ | a heading with nothing under it | whether what is under it is any good |
| ~unserved-rung~ | a rung above the areas that nothing was derived from | with ACT loaded, a principle that is governing conduct |
| ~unclaimed-time~ | hours no area is answerable for | hours nobody clocked at all |
| ~domain-gaps~ | a life sphere no area claims | a sphere you never put on the list |
| ~long-heading~ | width | whether it is a name or a sentence |
| ~past-its-date~, ~overdue-review~ | dates | whether the review was worth anything |

And one limit over all of them:

#+begin_quote
The tool can only see what was written as structure.
#+end_quote

A capability named in prose in the vision's body cannot be reported as
unbuilt, however clearly the prose says the vision requires it.  Nothing can
be inferred about something that was never made a rung.  Make it one and it
becomes visible; leave it prose and it stays yours alone to notice.

* The altitudes

The names are GTD's and are kept exactly, so that anything written about the
model can be read against this package.  Everything said *about* them — what
each rung is, how to find yours, how often to look — is written here from
scratch.

| Horizon | Name                              | Looked at                     |
|---------+-----------------------------------+-------------------------------|
|       5 | Purpose and Principles            | no schedule; when adrift      |
|       4 | Vision                            | no schedule; when adrift      |
|       3 | Goals and Objectives              | yearly, checked quarterly     |
|       2 | Areas of Focus and Accountability | monthly, and when life shifts |
|       1 | Projects                          | weekly                        |
|  Ground | Calendar/Actions                  | daily                         |

The line between this package and [[https://github.com/yoshzucker/org-foresight][org-foresight]] is not the altitude.  It is
the question:

| | asks |
|---+---|
| org-convect | *what is this for* — the ladder, and planning a project |
| org-foresight | *when does it fit* — the clock, the day, the deadline |

Which is why planning a project lives here even though a project is H1.

Note the top two rows.  *GTD gives them no calendar at all.*  A yearly deadline
for purpose would be this package inventing a rule and attributing it to GTD,
so ~org-convect-review-cadence~ covers only areas and goals.  What calls the
upper two is a signal; see [[*The ACT overlay][the overlay]].

* The data structure

** The ladder is declared, not located

An entry belongs to a horizon because it says so:

#+begin_src org
,** engineering
   :PROPERTIES:
   :CONVECT_HORIZON: area
   :CONVECT_SERVES:  A team that runs itself
   :END:
#+end_src

| Property           | Means                | Values                                  |
|--------------------+----------------------+-----------------------------------------|
| ~CONVECT_HORIZON~  | what this is         | ~area~ / ~goal~ / ~vision~ / ~purpose~  |
| ~CONVECT_SERVES~   | what it is in service of | names of higher rungs, ~;~-separated |

Nothing depends on the file or the section an entry sits in.  Sections are
scaffolding for the eye; split the ladder across files, move an entry out of
its section, and it still reads.

Three things follow, and each answers a problem that nesting could not:

- *two parents are ordinary.*  One goal frames several areas and one area
  serves several goals.  That is a graph, and Allen himself describes the
  horizons as altitudes to think from rather than a filing hierarchy.
- *the ladder can be written bottom-up.*  Nesting areas under values would
  require the top before the bottom, which is the opposite of the advice.
- *files are free.*  Work and personal ladders can live apart.

** Nothing points downward

A task carries a ~CONVECT_AREA~ and nothing else.  It is read with
inheritance, so a project marked once collects all its descendants, and a
five-minute job costs no thought about purpose to open.  Only the review climbs, and only the code
doing the climbing pays for it.

That is also where the upward evidence comes from: org-foresight's clock scan
walks the files once and totals per property, so asking it to group by
=CONVECT_AREA= gives time per area without a second pass.  Entries carrying no
area land under =?=, and that row is usually the interesting one.  The
dependency is soft both ways — the ladder reads without a clock, and the clock
reads without a ladder.

** Goals carry no TODO state

~org-agenda-files~ is usually derived from which files hold open tasks.  Give a
goal a TODO keyword and the ladder file joins the daily agenda, which is
exactly wrong for something GTD reviews quarterly.  Achieved and abandoned are
recorded as notes; tidying up is ~org-archive-subtree~.

** Reviews are notes, rungs are headlines

The test is one question: /will this be looked up, moved, or given a state on
its own later?/

| Thing                            | Answer   |
|----------------------------------+----------|
| a rung                           | headline |
| a choice point (its properties are a row in a column view) | headline |
| what happened to a rung this month | note   |

So *reviewing a rung is pressing =C-c C-z= on it and writing what you
concluded.*  Nothing new to learn.  The newest inactive timestamp in the
entry's own text is what ~org-convect-overdue-p~ reads — children excluded,
because recording a choice point under a principle is not the same act as
reviewing the principle.

** Which rung gets asked for next

~org-convect-next-rung~ returns the *lowest empty* one, and only that one.
While areas are unwritten it asks for areas, never for purpose.

An empty rung is the prescribed path, not a defect.  What would be a defect is
never being asked to climb off it.

* The ACT overlay

GTD says /when/ to revisit the upper horizons and never says /what to ask/
there.  The weekly review has a procedure; nothing above projects has an
equivalent.  Worse, the top two rungs are read when direction or motivation
has gone — a condition rather than a date, and one nobody notices from inside
it.  It is a trigger that never fires on its own.

ACT has the missing questions, and one of them makes that trigger observable:

**A principle whose last few choice points all read =away= is a principle that
alignment has quietly left.**

And GTD already treats the top rung as being partly about conduct — the
standards you hold, not only what you are for — so keeping a record of
behaviour against it is not a foreign idea grafted on.

** Domains are not areas

The overlay's =org-convect-act-domains= is a list of life domains, and it is
easy to mistake it for a second copy of the areas.  It is the opposite of that,
and the difference is what makes it work.

| | Domain | Area |
|---+---+---|
| What it aims at | *covering a life* | *being answerable* |
| Complete? | tries to cover everything | no — you are not answerable for all of your life |
| Overlapping? | yes (family and friendship, health and leisure) | preferably not, but nothing enforces it |
| Where it lives | a setting.  Not in the file | a heading in the file |

The check is three lines long: collect the =ACT_DOMAIN= of every area, compare
against the configured list, report the domains nobody claimed.

It only finds anything because the two lists are *independent*.  Derive the
domains from the areas and the question becomes "is there an area for every
area", which is true by construction and finds nothing, ever.

That matters more than it sounds, because areas have a systematic blind spot.
The advice for writing them is to copy them off documents that already exist —
a job description, an org chart, the household's division of labour — and
**nothing writes down your friendships or your rest**.  So the area list will
reliably miss exactly the parts of a life nobody documents, which are often the
first to go.  An independent list of domains is how those get seen.

One area names one domain.  Letting an area claim two would make coverage
easier to satisfy without anything actually being maintained: an area claiming
both family and friendship silences the friendship question while friendship
still has nobody keeping it.

*** How finely to cut them

ACT has no single canonical list.  The most widely used exercise is the
*Bull's Eye*, devised by the Swedish ACT therapist Tobias Lundgren and
rewritten by Russ Harris: a dartboard divided into four life domains, on which
you list your values for each domain and then mark how close your actual living
is to them — a hit in the centre meaning you are living fully by that value, the
outer ring meaning you are nowhere near.  It is an alignment exercise, and the
four domains are its scaffolding rather than a claim about how a life divides.

The four are work/education, leisure, relationships, and personal growth/health.
Splitting them is usually right *here*, because this check's power is exactly
its granularity:

| Bull's Eye | split for coverage |
|---+---|
| work/education | work, learning |
| relationships | family, friendship |
| leisure | leisure |
| personal growth/health | health |

Lumping family and friendship together as "relationships" lets one area for
family answer for both, and a gap in friendship never appears.  Take a
published list as a starting point and split it wherever you suspect you are
not looking.

Note what the canonical four do not contain: money.  In ACT's instruments money
is generally treated as a means to the domains rather than one of them.  You
can still be answerable for the household finances — that is an area — and it
can name whichever domain it actually serves.

** Recording choice points

A choice point is written as a *child of the rung it is about* — no property
names the value, because the parent says it:

#+begin_src org
,** Honesty
   :PROPERTIES:
   :CONVECT_HORIZON: purpose
   :END:
,*** Wanted to keep quiet about the estimate
    :PROPERTIES:
    :CREATED:      [2026-08-30 Sun 14:02]
    :ACT_STRUGGLE: 7
    :ACT_MOVE:     away
    :END:
    - what pulled  :: saying it would cost me their trust
    - away         :: put the report off a week
    - towards      :: show the working, now
#+end_src

Struggle is how hard the feeling was fought, not how strong it was: intensity
is deliberately not recorded, because tracking it invites wanting it lower,
and wanting it lower is the trap the practice is about.

** It comes out

Everything ACT contributes is prefixed ~ACT_~ in the file and lives in
~org-convect-act.el~.  Delete that one feature and the ladder still reads,
still resolves, still knows what is overdue — only the upper two rungs lose
the signal that calls them.  The test suite proves it by running without the
overlay loaded at all.

* The review

=M-x org-convect-review= shows the ladder, with what wants looking at marked.
Its rows are live: =z= writes your conclusion onto the rung, =RET= goes to it.

#+begin_example
  Horizons Review                          2026-10-05
    3 due, 1 called

  Purpose and Principles
    Am I still behaving like this?  A principle does not go out of date, so
    asking whether it is current asks nothing — the question that carries is
    whether it is being kept, and where it was not.
    Honesty                       called -- 3 away in a row
      I do not let a number stand that I know is wrong
      ↳ 12 choice points, 7 away

  Goals and Objectives
    Reached, still reachable, or no longer a goal?  All three are answers;
    leaving it sitting there is not.
    a team that runs itself       due 2030-12-31
      the rota has someone else on it
      ↳ 3 below

  Areas of Focus and Accountability
    Is the standard being met — and is this still yours to keep up?  GTD asks
    the second out loud: should you be answerable for this at all, and could it
    be delegated or dropped?
    engineering                   due
      reviews come back the same day
      no branch is older than a week
      the build is green when I leave
      ↳ 12:30 clocked · serves a team that runs itself

    z  write the conclusion here   RET  go to it   t  follow the links   r  redraw
#+end_example

Three lines answer one rung: what it is, what it says, and one line of
everything else that is known about it.  The tally used to be four lines — the
clock, what it serves, how many answer to it, whatever the overlay adds — and
those are the rows the standard is read in now.

** What to ask when you come back

GTD says *when* to revisit each altitude and never says what to ask there.  So
each section carries a question, and they are not the same question:

| purpose | am I still behaving like this?  A principle does not go out of date, so asking whether it is *current* asks nothing |
| vision  | is this still the picture, and has anything moved toward it |
| goal    | reached, still reachable, or no longer a goal — all three are answers |
| area    | is the standard met, and *is this still yours to keep up* |

The area's is GTD's own, and it is the one most easily left out: /should you be
responsible for this at all?  Can you delegate or drop it altogether?/  A
standard being met perfectly is still worth losing if it was never yours.

Reading them as one question — "has it changed, and if not am I following it?"
— gets the upper rungs wrong.  A purpose almost never changes, so the first
half asks nothing there and the second is the whole of it.  Lower down the
first half is live: the job changes, and an area with it.

** Two ways to order it

=t= turns the board into a descent: a purpose, then indented beneath it
whatever is in service of it, and so on down.

#+begin_example
  purpose Honesty                        no calendar
            say the number you believe
  area    ├─ engineering                 due
  │          reviews come back the same day
  area    └─ admin                       due
             the vendor list is current
#+end_example

The altitude moves into a column, because a descent cannot group by it: a link
may skip an altitude, so two rows at one indentation can be a goal and an area.
Depth says how far the justification runs; the column says how high the rung
is; neither can be read off the other.

The ladder is a mesh rather than a tree, so a rule decides where a rung is
*drawn*: under the first name in its own =CONVECT_SERVES= that resolves, and
among rungs of that name, the first in the file.  Everything else it serves is
named in the tally as =also serves=.

That rule and no other, because it is the only one that is local.  Ordering by
where a walk happens to arrive, or by which parent comes first in the file,
means adding an unrelated heading at the top moves rows that have nothing to do
with it.  So **the order of =CONVECT_SERVES= means something**: put the other
name first to move the row.

** Why the whole ladder

Because reviewing is not something a date gives permission for.  A cadence
says how often to come back, not that looking is forbidden until then — and
the relationships are worth seeing on any day, which is otherwise only
possible by reading the file and rebuilding them in your head.

So what is due is *marked* rather than filtered for.
=C-u M-x org-convect-review= leaves the rest out, which is the shape of a
monthly sitting rather than a look.

** Two ways a rung asks

They stay apart in the marking rather than in the layout, which puts the
difference on every row instead of in a heading.

Areas and goals have a cadence, so they say =due= or the date they come due.
The rungs above have none — GTD reads them when direction or motivation has
gone, which is a condition and not a date — so they say =no calendar= until
something notices the condition, and =called= when something does.

The count at the top says when nothing is watching at all, because "0 called"
and "nobody is looking" mean opposite things.

** What is shown beside a rung

Altitude decides which rungs come due.  What you see while reviewing one is
its *neighbourhood*: what it was raised for, what answers to it, what the
clock says, and — from the overlay — what actually happened underneath it.  A
standard read alone is a sentence; read beside the goal it serves and the
hours that went to it, it is a judgement you can make.

All of it on one line, marked =↳=, which is org-foresight's sign for /this
belongs to the line above/.  In a descent the tree already says what a rung
serves and what answers to it, so those drop out and only =also serves= — the
links the tree could not draw — is left.

** Keys

| =RET= | go to the rung |
| =z=   | write the conclusion onto it |
| =t=   | follow the links, or the altitudes |
| =r=   | redraw |

Only =t= and =r= are bound by this package; the rest is Org's own, acting on
the marker each row carries.

=t= is =org-agenda-todo= underneath, and taking it is a repair rather than a
displacement: a rung deliberately carries [[*Goals carry no TODO state][no TODO keyword]], so pressing it on
one of these rows would *give* it one.  =g= is left alone — it opens the
motions for a great many people, and a command on it would take the prefix and
everything under it away here alone.

* Planning a project

=M-x org-convect-plan= opens a space under the project at point — or behind
the agenda line at point, which is usually where you are when you notice
something needs breaking down.

#+begin_example
  * NEXT move the service off the old host
    - Purpose ::
    - Outcome ::
  **                        ← point lands here
#+end_example

Then it gets out of the way.  =M-RET= for the next thought, =M-left= and
=M-right= to organise them into a shape, =C-c C-t= on the ones you are
committing to.

** Why only two fields

GTD's planning model has five steps: purpose, outcome, brainstorming,
organising, next actions.  Only two of them leave anything behind.

Brainstorming and organising are *acts*, not fields.  The brainstorm is raw
material for the organising, and what the organising produces is the shape of
the outline itself — so a form with five blanks would be asking for three
things that have nowhere to sit, and two of them would stay empty forever.

** Why the brainstorm is headings, and carries no keyword

Because org-foresight says so, in as many words:

#+begin_quote
A heading with no TODO keyword gets no record at all.  That absence is the
answer to "is this a project": it is scaffolding, a place to put things, and
the outline is full of it.
#+end_quote

So a brainstorm can be as long and as wrong as it needs to be and not one
thought of it is counted as work anybody has taken on.  Only what you mark
becomes work.  And headings rather than list items because =M-RET= and
=M-left/right= are the best structure editing Org has, which is exactly what
organising is.

** What the line under the cursor is asking for

Guidance has three places it can live and each fails differently.  In the file
it goes stale, written once and never learning anything.  In a command it is
only there when you thought to run it.  In the echo area it is there while you
are filling the thing in, which is the moment the question matters and the
moment you are least likely to go looking.

So the fields, and the rungs of the ladder, answer to Eldoc:

#+begin_example
  - Purpose ::
    → Purpose -- why this is worth doing at all, and what should guide how

  ** work.dev            (a rung, in horizons.org)
    → Areas of Focus and Accountability -- Under each one, as plain lines,
      the standard: what "kept up" means here.
#+end_example

It is idle-triggered, so it does not compete with typing, and it returns
nothing anywhere it does not recognise, so other Org buffers are untouched.
=org-convect-eldoc= turns it off.

It does not replace the =:GUIDE:= drawers.  Those teach — the deciding
question, an example of each answer, where to look for yours.  This reminds.

* Three views, three questions

The board answers "what wants looking at".  Two more answer the questions it
cannot.

| Command | Question | Why the file cannot answer it |
|---+---+---|
| =org-convect-review= | what wants looking at | state, not text: due dates, clocked hours |
| =org-convect-lineage-show= | what is this finally for | links point *up* only, so what serves a rung is never written near it |
| =org-convect-history-show= | what has happened to this | scattered through the subtree in three shapes |

All three draw rows that are live, and each can be called on the row under
point in either of the others.

All three show a rung's prose whole.  The board used to glimpse it — the first
line, cut at 68 columns — on the argument that it carries every rung and cannot
afford more.  That was right while the board was a list of what wanted looking
at.  It is where the reviewing happens, and a standard cut to its first line is
enough to recognise and not enough to judge.

What the thread narrows is *how many rungs*, not how much of each.

** Following the cursor

On the board, moving the cursor lights up every rung linked to the one you are
on — what it serves, what serves that, and the siblings answering to the same
goal.  The mesh is the part of the ladder a file cannot show, and this is the
cheapest way to see it: no command, no second buffer, just what moves together
when you move.

A rung that lights alone is telling you something too: it connects to nothing.

The work is done once when the board is drawn, so moving the cursor is a hash
lookup and an overlay swap, and a keystroke that stays on the same row does
nothing at all.  =org-convect-highlight-lineage= turns it off if the movement
is more distracting than the links are worth.

The face inherits from =secondary-selection= rather than naming a colour.  It
means "marked, but not the thing you are acting on", which is what these rows
are — and a theme that has thought about that face has already thought about
this one.

** The thread

#+begin_example
  Thread through work.dev

  Goals and Objectives
    a team that runs itself   -- serves nothing above

  Areas of Focus and Accountability
    org.work
    work.people
    work.dev   <- from here
#+end_example

Everything linked to one rung, followed in both directions.  The board shows
the ladder whole, which is roughly what the file already looks like; this
shows one thread of it, which is not — reading the file tells you what a rung
serves and never what serves it, so a thread has to be reassembled by hand
from both ends.

=serves nothing above= is the useful part.  A purpose is allowed to serve
nothing, since there is nothing above it.  Anything lower that serves nothing
is a thread that stops, and seeing where it stops is how you find out that a
whole quarter of work connects to a goal and to nothing beyond it.

** The history

#+begin_example
  Honesty

    2026-09-29  reworded   Reworded from "こうたに愛情を伝える".  伝えること
                           より読むことが先だった
    2026-09-15  choice     away · 見積りの誤りを黙っていたくなった
    2026-09-02  reviewed   まだ効いている。ただし部下相手だと崩れる
#+end_example

Three things you might want to look back over — what was concluded about a
rung, when its wording changed, how the moments went — turn out to be one
thing recorded three ways in the same place.  So they are read as one stream
with the kind as a column, rather than as three views that would have to be
read side by side to recover the order.

A reword is told from a conclusion by the first line of its note, which this
package writes.  That is text matching and a person writing the same words by
hand would be mislabelled; the alternative is giving notes a property, which
would make them addressable when the whole point of a note is that it is not.

* Rewording a rung

=M-x org-convect-reword= is how a rung changes its name.  Not
=org-edit-headline=, and the reason is mechanical before it is anything else:
rungs are pointed at *by name*, so renaming one breaks every =CONVECT_SERVES=
that named it — silently, because a link that resolves to nothing shows up
only in a report nobody has run yet.

So it is one act with three parts: rewrite the heading, repoint everything
that referred to it across every file, and write down what it used to say.

#+begin_example
  ** こうたの状態を読む
  - Note taken on [2026-09-29 Tue 21:40] \\
    Reworded from "こうたに愛情を伝える".  伝えることより読むことが先だった
#+end_example

That last part is why this is a command rather than advice.  A ladder that
changes is not a ladder going wrong — understanding deepens, circumstances
move, and a principle nobody has ever reworded is usually one nobody has
looked at.  What is worth keeping is *that* it changed and why, and keeping it
on the rung means the next review reads it without going through a version
history to find it.

Version control is still worth having and is not in competition with this.  It
keeps fidelity; this keeps legibility, in the place the question gets asked.

* Seeing what is missing

=M-x org-convect-doctor= counts the ladder and lists its blanks.  It writes
nothing.

It exists because a heading with an empty body looks exactly like a heading:
the guidance says what goes underneath each rung, and the file has no way to
show that it is not there.  Properties would have made the blanks visible —
that is the usual reason to reach for them — but the thing that goes under a
rung is several lines of prose and nothing computes with it, which is the
opposite of what a property is for.  So the body stays prose and the counting
is a command.

It is a report and not a scold.  Several of the things it lists are fine to
leave: a rung can stay bare on purpose while you go up and fetch the goal that
says what it is for, and most areas serve nothing at all.  What it is for is
that you should be *choosing* to leave them, rather than not noticing.

Every row is live, because it is an agenda buffer and the row carries the
marker.  Nothing here is bound by this package except redrawing:

| =RET= | go to the heading the finding is about |
| =z=   | write a note on it                     |
| =v f= | follow — the rung opens beside the list as you move down it |
| =r=   | redraw, after you have fixed something  |

Follow starts off.  It is worth turning on while you work through the list,
because whether a finding matters is decided by looking at the rung and not at
its name — a rung you meant to leave bare looks exactly like one you forgot.

Only =r= is bound by this package.  =g= is left alone: it is a motion prefix
for a great many people — =gg=, =gj=, =gs= — and a command on it would take the
prefix and everything under it away in this buffer alone.

=r= is why the remedies are worth having.  Fix something, press =r=, and the
report is current, landing you back on the same rung rather than at the top.  A
report you have to re-run by name after every fix is one you stop consulting.

Under each group is the thing that answers it.  A report that names a condition
and not the command that resolves it leaves you to go and find the command,
which is how a finding comes to be read as scenery.

* Findings

~org-convect-findings~ reports:

| Kind                | Means                                            | Answered by                    |
|---------------------+--------------------------------------------------+--------------------------------|
| ~bare-rung~         | a heading with nothing written under it          | write it, or leave it on purpose |
| ~long-heading~      | a name that has become a sentence                | move the sentence to the body  |
| ~undated-goal~      | a goal with no ~CONVECT_BY~                      | ~org-convect-set-date~         |
| ~past-its-date~     | a goal still sitting there after its date        | close it, or move the date     |
| ~unserved-rung~     | nothing below points at it                       | ~C-u org-convect-link~         |
| ~unresolved-serves~ | it points above at a name that is not in the file | ~org-convect-relink~          |
| ~misaimed-serves~   | it points at a rung that is not above it         | ~org-convect-relink~          |
| ~duplicate-name~    | two rungs share a name, so pointing is ambiguous | ~org-convect-reword~           |
| ~unknown-horizon~   | a typo in ~CONVECT_HORIZON~                      | fix the property               |
| ~overdue-review~    | past the cadence GTD gives that horizon          | =z= on the row                 |

The three rows about links are worded alike because they are read together, but
the first two are *not* opposites.  ~unserved-rung~ is an absence — nothing below points
here.  ~unresolved-serves~ is a break — something does point, at a name no rung
answers to, which is a typo or a rename nothing followed.

The true opposite of the first, a rung that points at nothing, is deliberately
not a finding at all.  Most work is justified on its own — keeping the engines
running is what an area is for, and the company's chores are a real
accountability.  Asking every rung to name a purpose would turn an honest
answer into a defect.

** Nothing below points at it

The one worth saying more about, because it is the one with a command behind
it.  A purpose that nothing was derived from is either not being used yet or is
not really held, and the ladder cannot tell which — but it can say that the
link is missing.

Links only run upward: ~CONVECT_SERVES~ sits on the lower rung and names the
higher one.  So resolving this means writing a property on rungs *below* the
one the finding is about — which you cannot do standing where the complaint is,
unless the command comes to you.  It does:

#+begin_src
C-u M-x org-convect-link
#+end_src

Standing on the purpose, it offers every rung below it — each shown with its
altitude, because two rungs can be worded alike and which one you meant is the
difference between a principle and the goal that serves it — and writes
~CONVECT_SERVES~ onto each one you pick.  The direction of the data does not
change; only the end you are standing at when you say it, which for an upper
rung is the end where you know the answer.  It runs from a doctor row as well
as from the file.

~org-convect-declare~ deliberately does *not* ask this.  It marks headings in
bulk — paste thirty areas, mark them, run it once — and a link prompt there
would either ask thirty times or apply one answer to all thirty, and one goal
for every area is the exact mistake the goal guidance warns about.

** Points above at something that is not there

The other link finding, and the only one that is always a defect: a
~CONVECT_SERVES~ naming something no rung answers to — a typo, or a rung
renamed without its references following.

Neither of the other two commands repairs it.  ~org-convect-link~ only ever
*adds*, which is right for the ordinary case but here would put a second name
beside the broken one; ~org-convect-reword~ renames a rung, not the link
pointing at it.  So there is a third:

#+begin_src
M-x org-convect-relink
#+end_src

It offers the links this rung carries, marking the ones that resolve to
nothing, and asks what to point at instead.  An empty answer drops the link,
which is the honest repair when the thing it named is gone.

** How far up a link may run

Up, and as far up as you like.

GTD does not require an item to have a parent one level above it.  The horizons
are altitudes to look from rather than a tree — [[https://gettingthingsdone.com/2011/01/the-6-horizons-of-focus/][David Allen's own account]] says work
"can be approached from many altitudes", and describes the upper levels as
*driving*, *generating* and *surfacing* what is below rather than owning it.
Clarity higher up reveals what is missing lower down; it does not mandate a
one-to-one correspondence.

So skipping is ordinary.  An area of accountability that serves a principle
with no goal or vision in between is one of the commonest honest shapes there
is — /Parent/ is answerable to something you hold, not to this year's
objectives.  ~org-convect-link~ offers every rung above, not the next one up.

What has no reading is a link that does not run up at all: a rung in service of
something at or below its own altitude.  The commands cannot produce one — they
only ever offer what sits above — so ~misaimed-serves~ is about a
~CONVECT_SERVES~ written by hand.

*** Does everything have to be connected?

No, and the two directions are not alike.

Pointing *up* is optional and its absence is never a finding.  Most areas serve
nothing at all: keeping the engines running is what an area is for.

Being pointed *at* is the question, and only above the areas.  A rung up there
exists to shape what is under it, so a purpose that nothing was derived from is
either not being used yet or is not really held — and the file cannot tell you
which.  That is why it is reported as a question rather than a defect.

An unconnected purpose is therefore fine to *have* and impossible to *check*.
It becomes checkable the moment either something below points at it, or conduct
is recorded under it — which is why a rung with choice points under it counts
as in use even when nothing in the ladder points at it.  What a principle
produces is behaviour, not a lower rung, and the ladder is the wrong place to
look for its output.

* Getting started

=M-x org-convect-open= writes the frame the first time.  It is ordered highest
first, which is the direction the ladder is read.

Each section carries a folded =:GUIDE:= drawer — one line until you press TAB.
Inside, every paragraph is labelled with the question it answers, in a teaching
order:

| *What this is*        | what that rung is                              |
| *Where to find yours* | where to look, which is rarely inside your head |
| *What goes underneath* | what to write once the heading exists         |
| *One written out*     | a whole one, heading and body                  |
| *The test*            | the question that decides                      |
| *Telling them apart*  | one that is, and one that looks like it and is not |
| *How often*           | the cadence GTD asks for                       |
| *How the file works*  | which property holds what, where there is one  |
| *What to type*        | the commands, last                             |

The labels also mark where the register changes.  Everything down to *How
often* is about the thinking — what you are being asked for, and how to tell
whether you have it.  *How the file works* is about the file: which property
holds what, which command writes it.  Mixing the two is what leaves a paragraph
unreadable as either advice or rule, so mechanism is kept to the field labelled
for it.

Two of the others earn their place.

*What goes underneath* is the one that gets forgotten.  A rung is a heading so
that it can go on accumulating; a file of bare headings is a file where the
monthly look has nothing to be a look *at*.  For an area what goes underneath
is the standard — what "kept up" means, the thing you would notice slipping.

*One written out* is the one that answers fastest.  Every other paragraph
describes a rung, and a reader who has followed all of them can still be unsure
whether the standard belongs in the heading or under it.  Being shown one
settles it without a sentence:

#+begin_example
  ** engineering
     Kept up means: reviews come back the same day; no branch is older
     than a week; the build is green when I leave.
#+end_example

The heading and the body are all anybody types.  The properties are written by
the commands, and are left out of the examples so that nothing in them looks
like something to copy by hand.

Nothing reads those drawers back, so delete them once they are in the way.
=M-x org-convect-refresh-guides= puts the current wording back under every
section and touches nothing else — guidance written once at file creation is
guidance that cannot learn anything afterwards, and this one has already
learned twice.

There are two ways in, and which one fits depends on whether you are inventing
a rung or copying one.

=M-x org-convect-add= writes a single entry, guided.  It picks the horizon from
a list that says what each one is, defaulting to the lowest rung you have not
filled; it prompts for the name with a hint about where to find yours; and it
offers to point upward *only when there is something above to point at*.  That
last part is deliberate — being asked to name a goal before any goal exists
would make the bottom-up order feel like a mistake, and it is the prescribed
path.

=M-x org-convect-declare= marks headings you have already written.  Write or
paste them under a section, mark the region, run it: each one takes the horizon
of the section it sits under, so a paste spanning two sections comes out right
rather than uniformly wrong, and nothing is asked at all.  Headings that are
already rungs are left exactly as they are, and the section headings themselves
are passed over.

Name areas for the role or the function, never for the thing they concern.
"Children" is not an area — there is no way to keep children to a standard.
"Parent" is: there is a way you mean to do it, and you can tell when you are
not.  At work the org chart has usually named the functions already, so they
come out as functions (=procurement=, =engineering=); at home nobody has named
anything, so you have to say what you are answerable for, and they come out as
roles.

Start with the areas, and use the second way for them.  The single most useful
thing GTD says about areas is that you should not invent them: most of yours
are already written down in a job description, an org chart, or the household's
division of labour.  Reading them off is faster and more honest than thinking
them up — and reading a list off a document is a paste, not thirty answers to a
prompt, which is the whole reason =declare= exists.

The rest of an entry is typed underneath it, because a rung is a headline
precisely so it can go on accumulating — what the standard is, and what
reviewing it concluded.

#+end_src
